DanskPath

A daily Danish practice that knows which mistakes are yours — and sets each skill at its own level.

The problem it was built for

Adult learners in Denmark do not have one Danish level. They have six.

After half a year of evening classes you can read a Modul 4 text and still write like early Modul 3. You follow a podcast and then stall mid-sentence ordering coffee. Every general-purpose language app treats you as a single number on a single track, and sets that number from a quiz you took in week one.

Worse, they are built for people with no teacher. You have a teacher. What you lack is the other six evenings a week, and anything that remembers that sin versus hans is the thing you keep getting wrong — not in general, but on Tuesday, in a subordinate clause.

The premise. The useful thing an app can do here is not teach Danish. It is to know precisely where you are weak, choose today's work accordingly, and get harder exactly as fast as you do.

How you use it

One screen, once a day, around fifty minutes.

  1. Open it. There is no menu.

    The landing screen is today's session, already planned: five steps, each with the reason it was chosen — "0 af 18 klaret i dette modul — største hul lige nu". You are not asked what you feel like practising, because on a tired Tuesday that question is how practice stops happening.

  2. Grammar, every day.

    Fifteen items drawn from your weakest error categories at your current level, with nothing repeated inside a sitting. It is the daily constant because it is 35% of the module requirement and the place weak spots surface first.

  3. Then whichever skills have fallen behind.

    Reading, listening, writing, speaking, conversation, vocabulary and pronunciation rotate by how far each sits from its Modul requirement, weighted so nothing goes stale for more than about a week. Short texts and clips are stacked until the slot is genuinely full.

  4. Say how it felt.

    Grammar scores itself. Writing and speaking cannot be marked by any app honestly, so each closes with three buttons: For let · Passende · For svært. That answer moves tomorrow's level.

  5. Come back tomorrow to a different plan.

    Nothing you have already read or heard is served again. Every skill you practised has moved on its own ladder.

Each skill climbs on its own

This is the mechanic everything else serves. Seven independent ladders, each moving on that skill's own results, each pulling content from its own CEFR band. Grammar runs daily and climbs fastest; speaking runs twice a week and lags — which is exactly what a real learner looks like, and exactly what a single-number app cannot show you.

Why not Duolingo

01

It is aimed at an exam you are actually sitting

Modul 1 to 5, mapped to A1 through B2, with reading types drawn from the PD3 paper — opslag, officielt brev, debat, klumme. Not a gamified path that ends nowhere.

02

It drills your errors, not a curriculum

Every exercise carries an error code. Miss enough reflexive possessives and tomorrow's grammar is reflexive possessives — and the surprise test a week later is too.

03

Six skills, six levels, measured separately

The B1 reader who writes at A2 gets B1 texts and A2 writing prompts on the same evening. One number cannot express that, so the app does not use one.

04

It assumes you have a teacher

It does not try to replace a classroom or mark your essays. It fills the six evenings between classes, which is where fluency is actually won or lost.

What it does not do yet

Stated plainly, because a pitch that only lists strengths is not worth reading.

Reading texts are short. They average 100 words with around one comprehension question. At B1 and B2 that is thin, and it is the next real piece of work.
Difficulty has a ceiling. There are no B2-tagged grammar exercises yet, so the top of the ladder draws from the general pool.
Writing and speaking are self-assessed. Honest — no app can mark a spoken answer — but it means those two ladders move on your judgement, not a measurement.
It has not been used for a full week. Everything above is verified in the browser. None of it is yet verified by a learner finishing seven sessions.
